41.9%of Kerala's emigrants are Muslim, vs. 29.3% of its total population
98.5%of Muslim emigrants go specifically to the Gulf

Migration has shaped Kerala's economy for half a century, and the Kerala Migration Survey has tracked it since the 1990s. The most recent rounds show that emigration isn't spread across religious groups in proportion to population, and that Muslim emigrants in particular are concentrated in a narrow band of Gulf-based, service-sector jobs.1

Emigration skews away from Kerala's population mix

Hindus make up 53.2% of Kerala's population but only 35.2% of its emigrants, the largest gap of any group. Muslims are 29.3% of the population but 41.9% of emigrants, and Christians are 16.9% of the population but 22.3% of emigrants.

For Muslims, that migration means almost exclusively the Gulf

89.2% of Kerala's emigrants go to the Gulf. But 98.5% of Muslim emigrants do, compared with 86.8% of Hindu emigrants and 76.3% of Christian emigrants.2

What Muslim migrants do once they're there

Salesman is the single largest category at 23.9%, followed by driver (15%), cashier, peon or office staff (8%), construction labourer (7.1%), hotel staff (6.7%) and storekeeper (6.2%). Together these six account for 66% of Muslim migrants' occupations.3
