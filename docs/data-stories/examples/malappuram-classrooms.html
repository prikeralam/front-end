65 students a batch

That's the average Plus One batch size in Malappuram in 2025, nearly double the 36 students per batch in Pathanamthitta, Kerala's smallest, and well above the statewide average of 54.

Kerala added 3.87 lakh Plus One seats across 7,223 batches in 2025. But that capacity isn't spread evenly, and district-level enrollment data shows the same district, Malappuram, carrying both the heaviest classroom load and the thinnest access to the science stream in the state.1

The most crowded classrooms in the state

Malappuram enrolled 70,472 Plus One students across 1,080 batches in 2025, an average of 65 students per batch, the highest of any district and 11 above the all-Kerala average of 54. Wayanad (60) and Kasargode (60) are the next most crowded. Pathanamthitta (36), Idukki (43) and Kottayam (43) run the smallest batches in the state.

Fewer options in the science stream

Only 42% of Malappuram's Plus One batches are science batches, tied for the lowest share in Kerala with Kasargode (40%), against a statewide average of 51%. Kottayam has the most science capacity, at 63% of its batches, followed by Pathanamthitta at 58%.

Crowding and stream access move together

Plotting batch size against science-stream share across all 14 districts shows a clear pattern: the districts with the largest average batches are also the districts with the smallest share of science batches. Where the district hasn't been able to open enough batches to match demand, existing seats get stretched thinner and skew toward humanities and commerce rather than science, which needs labs and specialised staff to expand.

Where it's headed: seat demand, 2027-2036

Using 2012-2027 birth records for the district, the projected number of Plus One aspirants in Malappuram eases only slightly over the coming decade, from 84,265 in 2027 to 77,442 in 2036, an 8.1% decline. Demand rises through the early 2030s, peaking at 86,106 in 2033.2

Why this matters

Malappuram's Plus One pressure isn't a temporary bulge. Demand stays near or above 82,000 students a year through 2035. Combined with the most crowded batches and the thinnest science access in the state, that points to a specific gap: more science batches, not just more batches, in the district that needs them most.
